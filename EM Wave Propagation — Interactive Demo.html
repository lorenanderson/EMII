Polarization describes the orientation of the E-field vector as a function of the phase difference δ between orthogonal components. Three simultaneous views: end-on (along propagation), oblique (45° to line of sight), and side-on (perpendicular).

\[ \mathbf{E}(z,t) = E_x\cos(kz-\omega t)\,\hat{x} + E_y\cos(kz-\omega t + \delta)\,\hat{y} \]

When two EM waves occupy the same region, they superpose. The resultant field is the sum of the individual fields. Constructive interference occurs when phases align; destructive when they cancel.

\[ E_\text{total}(z,t) = E_1\cos(kz - \omega t) + E_2\cos(kz - \omega t + \Delta\phi) \]

At a dielectric interface, incident waves partially reflect and partially transmit. Fresnel coefficients depend on polarization (s or p) and angle. At Brewster's angle, reflected p-polarization vanishes.

\[ r_s = \frac{n_1\cos\theta_i - n_2\cos\theta_t}{n_1\cos\theta_i + n_2\cos\theta_t}, \quad t_s = \frac{2n_1\cos\theta_i}{n_1\cos\theta_i + n_2\cos\theta_t} \]

In a rectangular waveguide, only modes above the cutoff frequency propagate. Each mode (TEmn or TMmn) has a characteristic field pattern and cutoff wavelength λ_c = 2a/m for the dominant TE₁₀ mode.

\[ k_z = \sqrt{k^2 - k_c^2}, \quad k_c = \pi\sqrt{\left(\frac{m}{a}\right)^2 + \left(\frac{n}{b}\right)^2}, \quad \lambda_c = \frac{2}{\sqrt{(m/a)^2+(n/b)^2}} \]

In a dispersive medium, phase velocity v_p = ω/k varies with frequency. Group velocity v_g = dω/dk describes how a pulse envelope travels. Anomalous dispersion can make v_g > c.

\[ v_p = \frac{\omega}{k} = \frac{c}{n(\omega)}, \quad v_g = \frac{d\omega}{dk} = \frac{c}{n + \omega\,dn/d\omega} \]
